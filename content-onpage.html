Content & on-page

The complete inventory, the freshness picture, the technical issues, and the single biggest content opportunity: winning back the ICD-10 / diagnosis-code cluster as interactive tools instead of AI-replaceable articles.

Mentalyc vs competition: content scorecard

The honest summary up front: Mentalyc's individual pages are competitive or better (its mental-status-exam page is ~5,900 words and scores 95 on-page, beating Supanote's ~4,600/88). What lags is the content system: breadth, silo structure, schema consistency, and technical hygiene. That is what the competitors out-execute, and it is all fixable without rewriting the pages you already have.

The programmatic opportunity: re-tool the code cluster

The clinical-reference and code cluster is where Twofold and Blueprint grew. Sized honestly (deduped of query variants), the defensible mental-health code cluster is ~133k/mo across ~201 codes; a larger ~286k/mo pool is general-medical codes Twofold targets that a therapy product has no real reason to chase. The mistake would be to rebuild the mental-health subset as the same article text AI Overviews now answer. Build it as tools instead, which capture the same searches, resist AI-Overview replacement, demonstrate the product, and gate leads.

Internal linking + silos

The blog is weakly routed to commercial pages, and the worksheets/examples silos are not interlinked with them. Restructure into hubs so the reference authority Mentalyc already has flows to the pages that convert.

  • Hub pages for: AI note-taking, clinical documentation, treatment planning, assessments, and an AI-scribe comparison hub.
  • Cluster every reference article and tool under its hub; link siblings and up to the hub; link the hub to the matching product page.
  • Push descriptive internal links from high-authority reference pages into the BOFU pages with intent-matched anchors.
